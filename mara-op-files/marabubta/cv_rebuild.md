## PROFESSIONAL EXPERIENCE

### Creator & Architect @ Marabunta HPC Swarm (Parallel Research)
*Mar-2005 – Present (Nights & Weekends) | https://github.com/radiation1977/marabunta-core*

Marabunta is a fully decentralized, biologically-inspired High-Performance Computing (HPC) fabric built entirely in Rust. It eliminates centralized orchestration (such as AWS Batch or ParallelCluster), relying instead on an Epidemic Gossip protocol to manage distributed state and compute capacity across highly heterogeneous edge devices.

* **Engineering Reality:** Reconciling Last-Writer-Wins (LWW) CRDTs with strict financial escrow requirements and resolving deep concurrency issues—such as handling Rust DashMap lifetime constraints during network split-brain scenarios—are the primary architectural challenges. Built an Algorithmic Spot Market, native Android JNI cross-compilation, and a Pareto Financial Estimator.
* **The LLM Paradigm Shift:** I couldn’t believe how well Google’s Gemini-3.1- comprehended my biology-based architectural analogies. I decided to dig deeper, abandoning rigid algorithmic prompts and basing all my architectural requests on natural sciences—treating networks as mycelium, and node failures as cellular apoptosis. The responses were excellent. I gained higher code quality using simpler, higher-level prompts. Ultimately, these models were trained primarily to interact with humans; I discovered that they respond significantly better when I act human—expressing raw frustration and natural metaphors—rather than speaking to them like a rigid compiler.*
* **The Human Side:** Building a lonely architecture on nights and weekends tests your sanity. It forces you to look at your own flaws. I realized that my own stubborn need for control was bleeding into the code. When I tried to force the network to behave synchronously, it crashed. I had to learn to write code that forgives the network, mirroring the patience I fail at, but desperately try to cultivate in my own life. It became a profound, almost uncomfortable psychological exercise in letting go. My wife drags me off the computer every single day, and I (almost) always gladly comply.
